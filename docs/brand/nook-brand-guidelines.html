01

Mission

Capture first. Find clarity next.

A quiet place to think.

Most tools ask you to organize before you understand. Nook does the opposite — get the thought down, let structure arrive later, on its own time. It exists for the five minutes before an idea is ready to be filed anywhere.

Is
  • Thinking workspace
  • Reflection companion
  • Spatial canvas
  • Calm environment
Isn't
  • A task manager
  • A note dump
  • A productivity score
  • A feed
02

Personality

Calm

Never demands urgency. Nothing blinks, counts down, or asks to be checked again.

Curious

Asks one honest question at a time. Never interrogates.

Human

Written and built like someone is actually on the other end, not a system.

Thoughtful

Every default has a reason. Nothing is there because it was easy to build.

Imperfect

Straight lines are for rulers. A little wobble means a person made this.

Warm

Quiet doesn't mean cold. Closer to a good notebook than a good dashboard.

03

Design Principles

01

Breathing room over density — Notion

Generous space is a feature. It is never empty space waiting to be filled.

02

Craft over polish — Arc

A few considered details beat uniform shine spread everywhere evenly.

03

Clarity over cleverness — Linear

If someone has to think about the interface, the interface has failed — not them.

04

Imperfect over engineered — Excalidraw

A few degrees of asymmetry read as made. Perfect symmetry reads as manufactured.

05

Consistency over a clever logo

None of the references above lean on their mark. They lean on the same few decisions, repeated everywhere.

06

Motion Principles

  • Calm, not snappy

    Eases out gently. Never arrives faster than the eye can follow.

  • Breathe, don't bounce

    No spring, no overshoot. Settles the way a thought settles.

  • Motion means something

    Marks a real state change — captured, connected, cleared.

  • Stillness is allowed

    Reduced-motion respected everywhere. Quiet is a valid default.
